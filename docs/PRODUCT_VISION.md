# Product Vision

## The promise

**Purple Cougar** is a gentle, premium 3D browser play experience for a child
around three years old. The child meets a lovable **female purple cougar**,
discovers that her tethered ball is fun to touch, and gradually explores a
connected Adventure Park. The experience should feel like a short playable
animated feature: warm, physical, expressive, safe, and immediately
understandable without reading.

The north-star reaction is simple: the child laughs when the ball flies, asks
to hear Purple Cougar’s friendly call again, and wants to see what lies beyond
the Meadow.

## Non-negotiable child experience rules

- No losing, health, punishment, harsh failure sounds, timers, or precision
  requirements.
- No ads, purchases, external links, chat, accounts, or child data collection.
- No text carries essential meaning. Motion, sound, gaze, shape, color, and
  generous hit areas teach the interaction.
- A miss must still create a friendly response. The ball may be the visual
  invitation, but the interaction zone must be forgiving.
- The normal play screen is nearly empty. A persistent unexplained numeral is
  not acceptable progress UI.
- Play is curiosity, not competition. Education emerges from action and never
  blocks a child who answers differently.
- Parents control narration, prompts, music/sound, reduced motion, session
  reminder, and future language behind a simple parent gate.

## Purple Cougar

Purple Cougar is female by identity and performance, not by stereotype. She is
gentle, funny, curious, and expressive; she should read as a cougar through
silhouette, muzzle, ears, paws, long tail, movement, and attention—not through
human clothing or an imitation of another character.

Her personality is visible before she moves: breathing, a small weight shift,
ear twitch, tail flick, eye focus, and interest in the ball. She reacts to the
child and to the environment: wake, stretch, look, sniff, paw, chase, pounce,
surprise, contentment, and celebration. Her friendly “roar” is a playful game
call, never frightening; optional narration may accurately explain that real
cougars make calls, purrs, chirps, growls, and hisses rather than lion-like
roars.

## The ball is a co-star

The ball is a colorful, safe toy connected through a clearly padded/playful
tether. It has its own readable state: resting, wiggling, rolling, bouncing,
swinging, stretching the tether, floating later in water, and ringing or
activating safe objects. Purple Cougar looks at it and reacts to its motion,
tension, speed, collisions, and rest state.

The physical object must match the approved toy reference in the current
specification: a matte orange ball with thin curved green stripes, a small dark
knot/grommet at the top, a thin black floppy cord, and a red cuff with a black
Velcro patch. The visual treatment must still communicate a soft, playful toy,
not a restraint.

## Interaction grammar

| Child action | Immediate response | Longer-term meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tap Purple Cougar / generous scene area | She wakes, notices the child, or performs a short friendly reaction. | “She knows I am here.” |
| Tap the ball | The ball visibly responds with a safe, bounded launch/bounce and she follows it with eyes/body. | “The ball is fun to make move.” |
| Drag and release the ball | Clear stretch direction, controlled sling/flick, and tether reaction. | “I can pull and let go.” |
| Bounce repeatedly | Countable spoken/visual celebration, not an abstract high score. | “I made one, two, three things happen.” |
| Reach a staged object | Ramp, bell, vehicle, water, or animal gives a distinct reaction. | “The park responds to experimenting.” |

Exact ball-tap physics remains an intentional reconciliation point: the approved
rev-2 loop requires the ordinary skip to draw energy from the cuff/character
motion, while the master brief requires a child-visible ball tap launch. The
target contract is a **small capped launch for deliberate ball interaction**
and anchor-driven energy for ordinary skipping; it must be explicit in code and
tests, never an accidental direct impulse on every screen tap.

## First product: “Purple Cougar and the Bouncing Ball”

The Meadow is the full first story, not a teaser level. It proves the final
emotional, visual, animation, camera, tether, touch/mouse, audio, and education
direction before park expansion.

1. A polished transition opens into a beautiful Meadow.
2. Purple Cougar rests or sleeps.
3. A child tap wakes her; she stretches, looks to camera, and makes a warm
   sound.
4. The ball wiggles.
5. The child makes it fly, bounce, and swing around her.
6. She is surprised, tracks it, and paws at it.
7. Three bounces provide number/voice/particle/animation feedback.
8. A larger bounce activates a nearby ramp; the ball rolls down and rings a
   bell.
9. Purple Cougar makes a happy call and celebrates.
10. A distant train appears as a visual promise of the wider park. Free play
    loops without a failure state.

The Meadow includes grass, flowers, moving plants, hills, sky, soft blocks,
ramps, tunnel-like/under-over play opportunities, a bell, windmill/butterfly
style ambience, puddles, safe climbing shapes, and distant scenery only insofar
as each supports this loop. It does not add a functional pool, vehicle yard,
train ride, or animal collection yet.

## The later connected Adventure Park

The finished park is one connected place, navigated through visual landmarks,
movement, and eventually the train—not a text menu of separate worlds.

- **Meadow and Ball Playground:** the central invitation and ongoing ball play.
- **Splash Pool:** shallow cheerful water, floating toys, slide/fountains,
  splashes, buoyancy, and later swim/paddle behavior.
- **Vehicle Yard:** a small car, monster truck, work trucks, and other vehicles
  introduced only when each has a clear, high-quality interaction.
- **Train Loop:** an audible, visible train that arrives, can be tapped, and
  later connects established areas.
- **Animal Area:** a small set of lively friends, each with a recognizable
  sound, simple animation, optional spoken name, and one memorable response.

## Learning without interruption

Narration is optional, short, accurate, and connected to a just-observed event:
animal names/sounds, vehicle functions, colors, shapes, one-to-five counting,
big/small, fast/slow, under/over, in/out, near/far, wet/dry, heavy/light, and
cause/effect. Questions invite rather than grade: every answer produces a warm
result and play continues.

## Current versus target

The source now contains an in-progress first-slice director and Meadow
scaffolding alongside its three.js, procedural-quadruped, fixed-step-ball, and
synthesized-audio foundations. That is implementation progress, not proof that
the product promise above is achieved. No Chrome visual baseline was available
when this document was written, so it makes no current claim about the hero’s
lovability, camera feel, scene quality, or mobile performance. Those are target
acceptance criteria, not achieved facts.
